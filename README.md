## coderivet

Software engineer working on long-lived codebases — the kind where architecture spans generations of patterns, migration debt has to be paid down without stopping delivery, and the bugs that matter most live at boundaries the linter never sees.

Right now that means two worlds in parallel: a large Ember application where Classic and Octane patterns coexist mid-migration, and a modern React codebase built on feature-sliced architecture. Different stacks, same discipline — know where your boundaries are, watch what crosses them, and make change safe while delivery keeps moving.

## What I’m working on

Alongside day-to-day engineering, I’m developing [RecurseWright](https://rw.krupnyak.com/) — an evolving exploration of what happens when humans, AI, tools, methods, and engineering practice begin to change together.

The first full RecurseWright article is now public:

[**Experimental RecurseWright: Engineering on Moving Ground, and Other Pretty Problems with Stability**](https://rw.krupnyak.com/articles/experimental-recursewright-engineering-on-moving-ground/)

It follows a familiar engineering situation through changing evidence, decisions, tools, and boundaries — with two small interactive artifacts that let the reader step into the work rather than only read about it.

An earlier trace of the idea is here:

[What Does It Mean to Be Embarrassed to Use a Calculator?](https://dev.to/coderivet/what-does-it-mean-to-be-embarrassed-to-use-a-calculator-58fc)

The rest is still being built.

## On AI in this work

I use AI as a structured collaborator: code review, context retention, pattern recognition, and exploring engineering decisions across codebases too large to hold in working memory at once.

But the more interesting question is no longer simply how AI can help with the work.

It is what changes in the human, the tools, and the way of working as that collaboration evolves — and what needs to remain observable enough for us to keep judging the work responsibly.

That is part of what RecurseWright is investigating.
